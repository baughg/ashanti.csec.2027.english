## Weaknesses and the steps to fix them

**Summary writing.** Evidence: Copied whole lines from the extract, gave 2 of 5 points, 74 words against a limit of 60.

- Find the points first: one point per paragraph, in a numbered list
- Rewrite each point in your own words, then join them
- Count your words every time; cut adjectives and examples
- Course lessons: 14 (Summary Writing), 30 (Literary Summary), 41 (Persuasive Summary)

**Grammar and agreement.** Evidence: 'Me and Jayden climbs', 'she don't say nothing', 'the river come': Creole grammar in exam writing.

- Learn the 4 agreement rules; check every subject and verb pair
- Keep one tense in a paragraph; underline the verbs when you check
- Code-switch on purpose: Creole for dialogue, Standard English everywhere else
- Course lessons: 2 (Grammar for Clarity), 5 (Diction, Register, Standard English and Creole)

**Visual texts.** Evidence: Judged a bar chart by eye (chose the rise that 'looked big', +5, not the largest, +7); missed 2 table items.

- Read the title, the key and the units before the bars
- Put your finger on the row and the column; say the value aloud
- Compare with numbers: 'rose from 40 to 65'
- Course lessons: 13 (Reading Tables, Charts, Maps, Signs and Symbols), 38 (Advertising, Media and Visual Persuasion)

**Argument writing.** Evidence: Strong feeling, but no counter-argument and only one reason with evidence.

- Plan claim, three reasons, evidence for each, then a rebuttal
- Use one statistic, one example and one expert per essay
- Finish with a call to action that answers the question
- Course lessons: 40 (Building an Argument: Claims, Reasons, Evidence and Counter-arguments), 42 (The Persuasive Essay)

**Punctuation.** Evidence: Comma splices ('The water rose, we ran'; 'the roof was gone, the zinc was ...'); missing apostrophes.

- Find each sentence's main verbs; two main clauses need a full stop or a joining word
- Possessive check: 'whose is it?' means an apostrophe
- Course lessons: 3 (Punctuation for Meaning)
